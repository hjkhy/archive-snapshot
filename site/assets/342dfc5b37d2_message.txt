So a Jew is a person descended from the tribe of Judah, most simply. There is a religion, Judaism, that many follow, but unlike Christianity, it's not orthodox -- that is, if you don't believe in Jesus, you're not a Christian, but if you don't believe in God, you're still a Jew (there are a lot of atheist Jews, like myself). We are actually an ethnic group, dating back to about 1000 BC, and our ancestors originated in the Levant, in what is today southern Israel and the West Bank (Jerusalem was the center of the kingdom of Judah).

The causes of the diaspora are not 100% known -- there were some expulsions, some people who just moved for economic opportunity, same as any other situation -- but most of us ended up in various places around the world, primarily Central and Eastern Europe (Ashkenazi or "German" Jews; these are the vast majority of American Jews and I think a minority of Israeli Jews; these are the "white Jews"), Spain and Portugal (Sephardic or "Spanish" Jews, who got expelled in the Inquisition and are now largely from Greece and Turkey/former Ottoman Empire), and the Middle East (Mizrahi or "Eastern" Jews, in Yemen, Iraq, Lebanon, etc; they were mostly expelled to Israel in the 20th century). There are also smaller populations in other parts of the world, such as China (the Kaifeng Jews), Ethiopia (Beta Israel), Italy, and India. Note: there is a conspiracy theory saying that Ashkenazi Jews descend from Khazars, a Central Asian people, who converted to Judaism, and that we are not actually the descendants of our ancestors. This is supported by nothing in the scholarly literature, which shows (based on DNA) that we are about 50% European and 50% Middle Eastern, in line with the theory that some men came from Israel, converted European women, and then everyone married within the tribe after that generation.

The traditional religion of the Jews is Judaism, and you can convert to it, but as I mentioned before, there are atheist Jews. Truth is, we're not purely a religious group, or an ethnic group -- we're an ethnoreligious group.

As for Zionism. Zionism began as a secular political movement in the late 19th century to solve "the Jewish problem" -- that is, the problem of Jews being discriminated against and persecuted. (Note: that's what they said historically, but no one says this today, and it would sound very weird if you did.) It was started by secular European Jews -- look up Theodor Herzl. They believed that the solution to the Jewish Problem was for the Jews to have a state of their own, and they decided the best place was Palestine, our people's original home. Note: at this time, Palestine was just the name of that region; much like we might today refer to the Midwest in the US, which is comprised of multiple political entities. At this point in time, Palestine was part of the Ottoman Empire, which all the European powers were trying to get a slice of.

I'll skip over many of the details, but England treated the Jews as the French did the Catholics and the Russians the Orthodox -- that is, they considered themselves in charge of "protecting" us, even when we weren't from England but from other parts of Europe. So they often negotiated with the Ottomans on our behalf.

At its heart, Israel/Palestine is an immigration debate. The locals wanted to restrict immigration, we wanted to immigrate. There was a lot of push/pull, and conflict, because we didn't want to live like the Palestinians, and we wanted to set up self-sustaining (Jewish) societies. There was also issues of property rights -- while many Palestinians operated under communal, inherited land rights, where I work and live on the land my father worked and lived on, and so will my son, forever in both directions (or at least it feels that way), the Ottoman Empire in (I think) 1858 changed the law to more closely resemble Western law, where land can be bought and sold. And many Jews did by land, from those who owned it -- which were not the people who thought they "owned" it, that is, the people who worked it all their lives. With both sides thinking they had the right to the same land, there was conflict.

When the Ottoman Empire fell, Britain got control over the Mandate of Palestine, in which they variously allowed and restricted Jewish immigration. Then there was the war of 1948 when they left, and I'm realizing I'm getting way off track.

So what's the difference between the two? That's like saying what's the difference between black people and the civil rights movement. The civil rights movement exists for black people, but there were white people who participated, and black people who didn't. And black people are just a group.

Likewise, Zionism is a political organization/belief, that believes Jews have some right to Israel -- what exactly that entails differs wildly based on which specific ideology within Zionism you're looking at. But there are many people who aren't Jews, and are Zionists. And there are people who are Jews, but not Zionists.
